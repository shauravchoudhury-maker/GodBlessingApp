For guides

Somebody is writing about it tonight.

A bereavement. A diagnosis. A marriage coming apart. A faith that has gone quiet. They do not need a professional. They need someone who has stood where they are standing, and who will send one verse and a few honest words. That is the whole job.

Who we are looking for

Not credentials. Lived experience.

The Blessing Circle matches people on what a guide has been through, not what they have studied. Someone writing about losing their mother is sent to a guide who has buried a parent. Someone frightened by a diagnosis reaches a guide who has heard one. You do not need to be ordained, trained, or certain of anything. You need to have carried something, and be willing to sit with someone carrying it now.

You answer from your own tradition — Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Sikhism, Taoism, or no tradition at all — and in any of the languages you can write in. People who can only pray properly in the language they learned it in are the hardest to reach, and the most grateful to be reached.

What is actually asked of you

One message. In your own time. From your own home.

A request arrives in your queue. You read it, choose a verse from your scripture — we hold 832 of them — and write a few lines of your own. Then you send it. That is a blessing. Most take ten to twenty minutes, and there is no rota, no shift, no minimum. You can answer one and never come back, and nobody will chase you.

You cannot write to the same person twice unless they ask you to continue. That is not a limitation on you — it is what makes the whole thing safe, for them and for you, and it means you never have to wonder whether to follow up.

What the system refuses on your behalf
  • Your reply cannot contain an email, a phone number, a link, or an invitation to talk elsewhere — the send button will not let it through.
  • You cannot ask for money, and nobody can ask you for it. Guides are never paid and never pay.
  • You cannot tell anyone to stop a medication or skip a doctor. If you try, it is refused with an explanation.
  • You cannot sign with a title — no "Dr.", "Pastor", "Rev." — because a frightened person reads it as a credential we have checked.
  • You only ever see requests matched to you or offered to the whole circle. There is no queue to browse.

These protect the person you are writing to. They also protect you: you will never be put in the position of being asked for something you should not give.

What we check, and when

We are open about this because the people you write to see it too. Every guide's card shows what we have confirmed — and, with the same weight, what we have not.

Listener
Sends one blessing per request. Where everyone starts.
Needs: an application we have read, and a conversation with us.
Companion
Also receives requests from people in real distress, and can hold a conversation when the person asks for one.
Needs: identity confirmed (a passport or licence and a selfie, through Stripe — we never see the document), your first replies read and cleared, and two stewards who vouch for you. EverVerse pays for the check.
Steward
Can vouch for new guides and sit in, quietly, on conversations to keep everyone safe.
Needs: an affiliation we have confirmed by ringing the organisation, and a background check.

Nobody is ranked. A steward is not a better guide than a listener — they are cleared to carry more. There is no hurry to climb, and most guides never need to.

How to join, step by step
  1. Apply. Six short steps: who you are, which faith you answer from, what you have lived through, your languages, a few words about why, and three promises. Ten minutes. Start here →
  2. We read it. A person, not a filter. If you named an organisation, we will ring them — not to check up on you, but because "confirmed with St Mary's" on your card means something to a stranger only if we actually confirmed it.
  3. Confirm who you are. Sign in with Google on the guides tab and follow the identity check — a passport or licence and a selfie, through Stripe. We never see the document; we keep a date and a reference. EverVerse pays for it.
  4. You are in — for celebrations first. No interview, no waiting for a call. Your queue is there straight away, with everything you wrote on the application already on your profile. For your first few replies you are shown celebrations only: a birth, a wedding, good news. Real requests from real people, and the gentlest possible start.
  5. A person reads your first three. Not a filter — someone who has done this reads the replies you wrote. Then grief and the hard days reach you too. Either of us can say no at this point, without explanation.
  6. Every request after that. Read it twice. Choose a verse. Write as you would to a friend who had told you the same thing. Send it. That is the whole job.
Questions people ask
Do I need to be ordained, or trained?

No. Lived experience is what we match on, and it is the one thing that cannot be faked well. Clergy and chaplains are very welcome, and are held to exactly the same rules as everyone else.

How much time is it?

Ten to twenty minutes per blessing, when you have it. There is no minimum and no schedule. Twelve a day is the maximum, because this work goes badly when it is rushed.

Is it paid?

No, and it never will be. Guides are volunteers. If EverVerse ever takes donations they go to charities of each faith, and you will never know whether anyone you wrote to gave anything — that is deliberate.

What if I say the wrong thing?

You will be writing to someone who chose to hear from a person, not a professional. Honesty lands better than polish. The system catches the genuinely dangerous things before you send; the rest is grace, on both sides.

What if someone writes something that frightens me?

Requests that suggest real danger are shown local crisis lines before they reach anyone, and are routed only to companions and stewards — never to a new guide. You can also report any request with one tap, and a person reads it.

Can I stop?

Any time. Untick "available" on your profile and nothing more arrives. Nobody will ask why.

Which languages?

Twenty-four, including Spanish, Hindi, Urdu, Punjabi, Gujarati, Tamil, Bengali, Arabic, Persian and Hebrew. Requests are matched to guides who write the same language. If you can write in a language other than English, you are especially needed.

Will you use AI to write my replies for me?

Never. No blessing on EverVerse is written or suggested by a machine, and we say so to every person who asks. Your words are the entire point.

Ready?

Ten minutes to apply. A person will read it. Thank you for even considering it.
