WHY ORGANISATIONS AND NOT ADS
-----------------------------
A guide has to be a person we can check. An organisation gives us three
things an ad never will: people who already do this work unpaid, a phone
number we can ring to confirm an affiliation (which is what puts
"confirmed with St Mary's" on a guide's card), and — the part that unblocks
each country — one person who can verify the crisis line and one native
speaker who can read the crisis patterns. Every email below asks for all
three. That is the whole campaign: ten guides, one hour, one name.

THE RULES BEFORE ANY EMAIL GOES OUT
-----------------------------------
1. Send from team@eververse.org, signed "EverVerse Team". Never a personal
   name (safeguarding.html, 13 Sep 2026).
2. Every sentence passes checkCampaignCopy() in campaign.js and
   checkGivingCopy() in giving.js. Run the final text through both before
   the first send: no clinical words, no numbers we cannot show, no
   urgency, no promises about outcomes, no "AI-powered" anything.
3. Do not promise that the Circle is open in their country. It is not.
   Say what it needs (regions.js says exactly what, per country) and ask
   for it.
4. Never list an organisation on the site as a partner until there is a
   written agreement (giving.js PARTNERS rule). An email reply is not one.
5. Contact addresses: NOT in this file, on purpose. Every organisation
   below has an official website with a contact page or a named office;
   find it there the day you send, because addresses go stale and a
   guessed one bounces or, worse, lands with the wrong person. Do not
   scrape or buy lists.
6. One organisation, one email, one follow-up after 10 days, then stop.
   Log every send in the table at the bottom. Reply to every reply within
   two working days, as a person.
7. If an organisation asks "is it paid?" the answer is on guides.html and
   it is No. If they ask for money, the answer is also no — we take
   nothing from gifts and pay nobody. If they offer help with helpline
   verification or a native-speaker review, say yes before anything else.

Week 1: US, IN, PH (English reaches furthest; three faiths).

per country in the log. The campaign is working when applications7d is
above 1 and stays there.

THE EMAIL (English)
-------------------
From:    EverVerse Team <team@eververse.org>
Subject: One of your people, one blessing — a volunteer ask from EverVerse

Dear [office / name of the ministry or council],

Someone in your tradition is going to write, tonight, about the worst
thing in their life — a death, an illness, a marriage ending, a faith gone
quiet — and hope that a real person writes back.

We run the Blessing Circle at eververse.org: a free service where an adult
writes anonymously, and a checked volunteer from their own faith writes
back once, with a verse from their own scripture and a few honest words of
their own. No account, nothing to buy, and no AI writes a single word — we
say that on our front door.

We are opening it in [country] and we would rather do it with you than
around you. Three asks, in order of size:

  1. Ten people in your community who have carried something and could
     each write one blessing. Not clergy necessarily. Ten minutes to
     apply, and a person reads every application: eververse.org/guides.html

  2. One hour from one person who lives in [country]: to check that the
     crisis helpline we show — [line as in world.js] — is the right one,
     and to read the phrases we use to recognise crisis in [language].
     We will not promote the Circle in [country] until both are done.

  3. If a volunteer says they serve with you, may we ring you to confirm
     it? Their card then says "confirmed with [organisation]" to the
     stranger they write to.

What we promise is on one page, written for exactly this conversation:
eververse.org/partners.html. Every guide confirms their identity before
their first reply (we pay for the check); a new guide sees only
celebrations until a person has read their first three replies; a reply
cannot contain a phone number, a link or a request for money; and anyone
can report anything in one tap, to a person. Guides are not paid and the
person asking never pays. Each guide can print a signed letter of service.

If any of the three is possible, reply to this address and a person will
answer. If none is, thank you for reading this far.

EverVerse Team
team@eververse.org · eververse.org/partners.html

Asunto: Una persona de su comunidad, una bendición — una petición de voluntariado de EverVerse

Estimados [oficina / nombre]:

Esta noche alguien de su tradición va a escribir sobre lo peor que le está
pasando — una muerte, una enfermedad, un matrimonio que se rompe, una fe
que se ha quedado en silencio — con la esperanza de que una persona real
le responda.

En eververse.org tenemos el Círculo de Bendiciones: un servicio gratuito
donde un adulto escribe de forma anónima y un voluntario verificado de su
misma fe le responde una sola vez, con un versículo de su propia escritura

que ninguna inteligencia artificial escriba una sola palabra.

Queremos abrirlo en México con ustedes. Tres peticiones, de mayor a menor:

  1. Diez personas de su comunidad que hayan pasado por algo difícil y
     puedan escribir una bendición cada una. No hace falta ser clérigo.
     Diez minutos para aplicar; una persona lee cada solicitud:
     eververse.org/guides.html
  2. Una hora de una persona que viva en México: para confirmar que la
     línea de crisis que mostramos — SAPTEL, 800 911 2000 — es la correcta,
     y para leer las frases con las que reconocemos una crisis en español.
     No promoveremos el Círculo en México hasta que ambas cosas estén hechas.
  3. Si un voluntario dice que sirve con ustedes, ¿podemos llamarles para
     confirmarlo?

Lo que prometemos está en una página: eververse.org/partners.html. Los
guías no reciben pago y quien escribe nunca paga.

Si alguna de las tres es posible, respondan a esta dirección y una persona
les contestará.
