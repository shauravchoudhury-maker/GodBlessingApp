EverVerse — the TIP framework, the giving engine, and the "wallet" question
===========================================================================
Design and thinking, 13 September 2026. The model itself is in giving.js and
is unchanged: EverVerse takes NOTHING from a gift; a tip to EverVerse is a
separate, optional, never-preselected choice. This document is about how
that becomes an engine that moves real money in nine countries without
breaking any promise we have made — and what "a secure digital wallet"
should and should not mean for us.


0. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION
----------------------------
EverVerse must never hold anyone's money. Not the giver's, not the
charity's, not a "balance". The charity is the merchant of record for the
gift; the tip is a separate line that reaches EverVerse as a platform fee;
a licensed processor holds the funds in between. "Wallet" means the
giver's own wallet — Apple Pay, Google Pay, PIX, UPI, M-Pesa, GCash, GoPay —
which is how people in the nine countries actually pay, and which we accept
through the processor. It does not mean an EverVerse wallet, because an
EverVerse wallet would make us a money transmitter in every country we
serve, and a target in all of them.


1. WHAT A TIP IS, PRECISELY
---------------------------
A tip is money a giver chooses to add FOR EVERVERSE, at the moment of a
gift to a charity, after the gift amount is chosen, with 0 as the default.
It is never a share of the gift (PLATFORM_SHARE = 0). It never depends on
whether anyone answered them, who answered, or what was said (G6, guard
rail 1 in giving.js). It is never asked for on the wounded side — not on
the ask page, not in a reply, not in a thread (mayAskToGive). It pays for a
listed set of things (PLATFORM_SHARE_PAYS_FOR) and the list is printed.

What a tip is NOT, and must not drift into:
  · a payment for a blessing ("thank your guide" — refused by NEVER_IN_GIVING)
  · a subscription, a premium tier, or credit that buys anything
  · a donation to a charity (that is the gift; the tip is separate in law
    and in the sentence the giver reads)
  · a stipend for guides — shelved until the December 2026 reassessment
    (guide-support.js REASSESS_ON). If it comes back, tips can fund it,
    flat and monthly, never per reply.


2. THE WALLET QUESTION — THREE READINGS, ONE ANSWER
---------------------------------------------------
"A secure digital wallet" can mean three different things. Two of them we
should refuse.

  A. AN EVERVERSE WALLET (custodial). Givers top up a balance; charities
     accumulate a balance we pay out. REFUSE. Holding funds for others is
     money transmission: FinCEN MSB registration plus state-by-state money
     transmitter licences in the US; FCA e-money authorisation in the UK;
     RBI PPI licence in India; BCB in Brazil; CBN in Nigeria. Each is
     months and six figures, and each makes us the thing attackers want.
     It also breaks guard rail 2: money sitting in a balance is money not
     yet accounted for.

  B. A CRYPTO WALLET. REFUSE for now. Anonymity is attractive on paper,
     but a charity cannot receipt it, the giver cannot deduct it, regulators
     treat it as A with extra steps, and "send crypto to a prayer app" is
     the sentence that ends trust. Revisit only if a partner charity asks.

  C. THE GIVER'S OWN WALLET, ACCEPTED THROUGH A LICENSED PROCESSOR. This
     is the answer. The processor is the wallet's counterparty and the
     licensed money transmitter. We hold nothing, see no card or account
     number, and receive only the tip, settled to our own bank account like
     any business. The nine countries each have a dominant wallet rail, and
     "give in the way you already pay" is the whole localisation story.

So the engine's first rule, above everything else:

     NO CUSTODY. EverVerse never holds a gift, a balance, or a payout.


3. HOW THE MONEY MOVES (ONE GIFT, ONE TIP)
------------------------------------------
Charity is merchant of record. EverVerse is the platform. Concretely, on
Stripe Connect (the reference design; other processors map to it):

  1. The charity onboards once as a connected account (Express). They pass
     KYC with the processor, not with us. They receive payouts from the
     processor to their own bank. We never touch the gift.
  2. The giver opens the giving page (calm surface only) → chooses faith →
     sees the partner charity by name (never before an agreement is
     signed) → chooses an amount → sees the disclosure sentence (giving.js
     disclosure()) with every cent named → chooses a tip (default 0) →
     chooses whether to cover the card fee (default yes).
  3. Hosted checkout (Stripe Checkout), never our own form. A DIRECT CHARGE
     on the charity's connected account for (gift [+ fee if covered]), with
     application_fee_amount = the tip. The processor takes its fee from the
     charge; the charity receives the gift; the tip lands in EverVerse's
     platform balance and settles to our bank.
  4. The receipt comes from the processor / the charity, not from us. We do
     not store the giver's email. Tax-deductibility is the charity's
     statement to make, never ours (NEVER_IN_GIVING already refuses it).
  5. A webhook (signature verified) writes ONE ledger entry: numbers and
     codes, no identity. See §5.
  6. Monthly, the transparency numbers are computed from the ledger and
     shown: gifts to charities, processor fees, tips received, what the
     tips paid for. Health shows them daily; the yearly report (L7) is
     built from the same rows.

Why direct charge + application fee rather than "we collect and forward":
  · it is the structure that keeps us out of "commercial co-venturer" and
    "commercial participator" law (giving.js guard rail 2) — the charity
    takes the donation; we charge a platform fee that the giver chose;
  · gift funds never sit in our balance, even for a day;
  · one refund path (the charity's), one dispute path (the processor's).

What a tip pays for, printed in the same words everywhere
(PLATFORM_SHARE_PAYS_FOR): identity checks for guides, the people who read
reports, hosting and crisis-line partnerships, translating the safety
checks. tips.js turns the month's tips into "N identity checks funded" so
the sentence can be a number, not a mood.


4. THE NINE COUNTRIES — RAILS, NOT WALLETS WE BUILD
---------------------------------------------------
Each starter country has a dominant way people actually pay. The engine
accepts it through a licensed processor; we never integrate a wallet
directly. Processors: Stripe (US/BR/MX + cards everywhere it operates),
Paystack (Stripe-owned; NG/KE/ZA/GH), Razorpay (IN — Stripe India is
invite-only and domestic-focused), Xendit (ID/PH). See tips.js RAILS for
the table. Three things the table hides:

  · INDIA — FCRA. A foreign gift to an Indian charity requires the charity
    to hold FCRA registration and use its SBI New Delhi FCRA account.
    Indian givers → Indian charities is domestic and fine (UPI via
    Razorpay). Diaspora givers → Indian charities is FCRA territory. The
    engine must route by the GIVER'S country as well as faith, and must
    refuse (with a plain sentence) a cross-border gift to a charity that
    cannot receive it.
  · CURRENCY. Gifts are taken in the giver's currency; the charity receives
    its own. The disclosure sentence prints both when they differ, and the
    processor's FX fee is named as part of "the card processor's fee".
    Never quote a conversion we did not get from the processor.
  · TIPS ACROSS BORDERS. The tip is a platform fee; it settles to
    EverVerse's home-country account in the processor's currency. Fine
    everywhere Stripe/Paystack operate. In India the fee has GST
    implications — the accountant conversation (L4) before the first
    Indian tip.


5. THE LEDGER — NUMBERS ONLY, APPEND-ONLY
-----------------------------------------
Firestore: blessing/circle/giving/ledger/{processorPaymentId}
  { at, kind: "gift"|"refund"|"dispute", gift, tip, feeProcessor,
    coversFees, currency, charityId, faith, giverCountry, rail, processor,
    status }
Amounts are integers in minor units. No name, no email, no card, no
address, no IP — the processor holds all of that under its own regulation.
Written ONLY by the webhook worker's service account (rules: create if
false to everyone; update/delete if false to everyone including admins —
corrections are new entries of kind "refund"/"adjust", never edits).
Read by admins only; the roll-up (tips30d, giftsToCharity30d, feeTotal,
byFaith, byCountry) is written into stats/latest so Health and the daily
check see it without a person signing in.

Reconciliation: once a day the worker asks the processor for the day's
balance transactions and compares to the ledger. Any difference is a
finding on the daily check, not a silent fix.


6. SECURITY — WHAT "SECURE" MEANS HERE
--------------------------------------
  · No card or account data ever reaches eververse.org (hosted checkout).
    PCI scope: SAQ A, the smallest there is.
  · Webhook signatures verified; events idempotent by processor id; replay
    of an old event writes nothing.
  · Amount bounds enforced server-side: MIN_GIFT, MAX_GIFT, tip ≤ 15 % of
    gift (TIP_OPTIONS). A tip larger than the gift is refused as a mistake.
  · Card-testing defence: processor's fraud tools (Radar) on; App Check on
    the page (A5); the rate-limit worker (A6) in front of checkout session
    creation; one session per browser per minute.
  · Secrets: processor keys live only in the worker; the page holds a
    publishable key and nothing else (S1–S5 pattern).
  · The worker's service account can write ledger + stats and nothing
    else (S5 — the split we already owe).
  · Kill switch: GIVING_ENABLED in the worker; flipping it makes the giving
    page say "paused" within one page load. Same shape as guide suspension:
    no deploy needed.
  · Disputes: the charity's, because it is the merchant of record. We show
    the processor's dispute rate on Health; over 0.5 % is a finding.


7. WHAT MUST BE TRUE BEFORE THE FIRST TIP
-----------------------------------------
In order. None of these is code.
  1. An entity. Today a tip would be personal income to the founder. L4
     (accountant + charity lawyer) is the gate, and it decides whether
     EverVerse is a company that takes tips or a nonprofit that takes
     donations — the sentence on the giving page differs.
  2. One signed partner charity (B2), onboarded as a connected account.
     PARTNERS in giving.js stays empty until the agreement exists.
  3. PROCESSING set to the real, signed rate, because the disclosure
     sentence prints it (LAUNCH.txt).
  4. A processor account with Connect enabled, in the entity's name.
  5. The ledger rules and the webhook worker deployed and reconciled for
     one week on test mode.
Then: US only, cards + Apple/Google Pay, one charity, one faith. Then
the next faith. Rails per country come after the first month reconciles.


8. WHAT THIS IS NOT (SO IT STAYS SMALL)
---------------------------------------
Not a fundraising platform: charities do not run campaigns here; givers do
not pick projects; there are no goals, thermometers or leaderboards. Not a
subscription: recurring gifts may come later, as the processor's feature,
never as an EverVerse balance. Not a way to pay guides — that decision is
December's. Not visible to a person who has just written about their
mother.


9. ROADMAP
----------
  Phase 0 (now → first charity signed): L4 conversations; entity; processor
     account; tips.js model in the repo (this PR); ledger rules written.
  Phase 1 (first charity): giving page live, US, cards/wallets, Checkout,
     webhook worker, ledger, reconciliation, transparency numbers on Health.
  Phase 2 (three charities, two faiths): FX + giver-country routing; PIX
     (BR), SPEI/OXXO (MX); FCRA gate for India.
  Phase 3 (Africa/Asia starters green): Paystack (NG/KE/ZA), Razorpay (IN),
     Xendit (ID/PH). Each rail goes live only in a country that is green on
     the Regions panel — we do not take money where we cannot yet answer.
  Phase 4 (December 2026): the guide-payback reassessment, funded from
     tips if at all, flat and monthly, per the shelved guide-support design.

Requirements added: M1–M6 in requirements.js (area "money").
