Someone in your tradition is writing about the worst night of their life. We would like one of your people to write back.

The Blessing Circle is a free service where an anonymous adult writes what they are carrying — a bereavement, an illness, a marriage ending, a faith gone quiet — and a checked volunteer from their own faith writes back once, with a verse from their own scripture and a few honest words of their own. Nothing to buy, no advertising, no AI. This page is what we would say to you across a table.

The vision, in three sentences

Nobody who reaches out in the language they pray in should hear nothing back.

The apps people already use for comfort either have no human in them, or charge by the minute for one. We think the person who has buried a parent, or sat in a doctor's office and heard bad news, or lost their faith and found it again, is the right person to write to someone going through it now — and that they exist in every congregation, and have never been asked.

So the Circle is built around one thing: a real person, from your tradition, writing in their own words, for free. Everything else on this page exists to keep that safe.

How it works
  1. A person writes. Anonymously, no account, in any of thirty-five languages. They choose their tradition and what it is about. Before it is sent, it is checked for signs of crisis or of a writer under 18; either way the person is shown a helpline for their country first.
  2. It goes to one guide. Matched on tradition, language, and what the guide has lived through — a request about losing a mother goes to someone who has. Never to a queue anyone can browse.
  3. The guide writes back once. One verse from their own scripture, a few lines of their own. Ten to twenty minutes. They cannot write again unless the person asks for a conversation, and then only in turn, with a steward reading alongside.
  4. The system refuses the dangerous things. A reply cannot contain a phone number, an email, a link, a request for money, or advice to stop a medication. The send button will not let it through. Nothing is edited or deleted afterwards, so a report is read against the actual words.
What we ask of you
Ten people who could each write one blessing.

Not clergy necessarily — people in your community who have carried something and would sit with someone carrying it now. Share the guides page with them; applying takes ten minutes and a person reads every application.

One person to spend an hour with us on your country.

We show a crisis helpline to anyone who writes in real distress. We will not promote the Circle in a country until someone there has checked that the number we show is right, and until a native speaker has read the phrases we use to recognise crisis in their language. An hour, once.

Optionally, a name we can confirm.

If a guide tells us they serve with you, we ring you to confirm it — and their card then says "confirmed with [your organisation]" to the stranger they write to. It means something only because we actually checked.

What we promise, and what we do not
We promise
  • No AI writes, suggests or edits a blessing. Ever. It is on our front door in the same words.
  • Every guide confirms their identity with a document and a live selfie before their first reply. We pay for it; we never see the document.
  • A new guide sees only celebrations until a person has read their first three replies. Grief never reaches an unread guide.
  • Anyone can report any reply in one tap, to a person. Our safeguarding policy sets out every step and the times we measure.
  • Your volunteers are never asked for money and never told whether anyone gave any. Gifts from the public go to charities of each faith; we take nothing from them.
  • A signed letter of service for every guide, on request — facts only: months, blessings written, standing.
We do not
  • Pay guides, or charge the person who asks. It is free on both sides, and we say so.
  • Offer anything clinical, or let a guide pretend to. Guides are volunteers offering prayer, scripture and reflection, and their card says plainly what we did not check.
  • Let a guide write to the same person twice uninvited, or reach them anywhere else.
  • Rank guides, rate them, or show anyone a league table.
  • Promote the Circle in your country before your line is verified, your language is reviewed, and three guides can answer in it.
What your volunteers get

Every check paid for. A record of service kept as facts and printed as a signed letter whenever they ask — seminaries, chaplaincy programmes and employers accept it. A monthly call with the other guides, and a steward who reads their replies and writes back. And the thing that keeps most people: a stranger, on a bad night, heard from someone who had been there.
